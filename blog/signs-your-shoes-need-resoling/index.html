7 Signs Your Shoes Need Resoling

If you ignore the early signs, a KES 1,500 resole becomes a KES 12,000 shoe replacement.

Most people wait until their shoes are falling apart before bringing them in. By then, what should have been a simple resoling has turned into a structural repair — or an unwearable shoe.

1. The tread has gone smooth

Run your thumb across the sole. If the tread pattern has flattened, especially at the ball of the foot, you have grip loss. In Nairobi's rainy season, smooth soles are a genuine slip hazard.

2. A hole has formed

Once you can see daylight through the sole, water damage accelerates fast. The insole soaks up rain, the shoe loses shape and the upper can start delaminating from the midsole.

3. The sole is separating from the upper

If you can slide a fingernail under the front edge of the sole, the cement bond has failed. This is a stitching repair, not a resole — catch it early before the gap grows.

4. You feel the ground through the sole

Thin soles transmit every stone through your foot. Not only uncomfortable, but it signals the sole has compressed past the point of no return.

5. Cracks in the sole

Common in Nairobi's dry months, when rubber and leather soles dry out. Small cracks become bigger cracks, and eventually a full split.

6. Water seeps through quickly

Any sole will let a little water in during heavy rain. But if your feet get wet within 30 seconds of stepping in a puddle, the sole is done.

7. Uneven wear

If one side of the sole wears faster than the other, your gait is putting uneven stress on the shoe. Resoling plus heel adjustment can correct this before it damages the structure.

Why Nairobi's climate matters

Nairobi's dry season dries out leather and rubber, causing shrinkage and cracking. The rainy season then soaks everything. This wet-dry cycle accelerates sole wear more than in consistently dry or wet climates. If you walk to work daily, expect to resole every 12–18 months.

The economics

A good pair of leather shoes costs KES 8,000–25,000. A resole costs KES 1,500–2,800. Even if you resole every year, you're still far ahead of replacing shoes every other year. And the shoe moulds to your foot — that comfort can't be replaced.
